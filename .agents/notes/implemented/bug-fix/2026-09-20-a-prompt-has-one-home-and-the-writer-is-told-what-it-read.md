# Agent Note: A prompt has one home, and the writer is told what it read

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-09-20-a-prompt-has-one-home-and-the-writer-is-told-what-it-read.zh.md)

## Problem

Two problems with one owner: the text the pipeline puts in front of a model.

**The text had no home.** Seven steps compose an instruction for a model — A1, A2, A3, B1, D2, D3, D4 — and every one of them was an inline template string inside `runner/registry.ts`, a file of five thousand lines. The rules those instructions state live in modules: `argument-plan.ts` says what a section owes its reader, `citation-bindings.ts` says what a citing sentence may claim. The text that TELLS a writer about a rule therefore sat in the file that runs the steps, which is how a constraint gets enforced in one place and stated in another — and nothing pinned any of it, in a package whose premise is that what reaches a model is recorded and reviewable.

**D3's writer was not told what the cited text was.** A driven run refused D3 with three objections; the first was about an attribution:

> The passage confirms the first-order-lag representation, its detrimental effect on safety, and the critical lag value above which safe CCC gains do not exist, but it was read in arXiv:2409.06884v1 rather than in the cited work, so the sentence's attribution of those findings to `cite-10.1109/tits.2026.3727135` is not established.

The run read the author's arXiv preprint; the bibliography credits the published article's DOI. `CorpusSource.accessNote` states exactly that — "read from the author preprint (arXiv:2409.06884v1) of the published article; the DOI is the article of record" — and did not reach the writer. This is the third time D3 stopped on material the writer was never given: a roster carrying a source's VERDICT without its passage (fixed in the [passage note](2026-09-20-the-writer-is-shown-the-passage-it-cites.md)), and before that an instruction carrying a constraint with no material behind it. The assessor was right each time.

## Decision

**The seven prompts live one module per step under `src/runner/prompts/`, over a shared skeleton.** `prompts/skeleton.ts` states what an instruction is assembled from and nothing about any step: five parts — the task, what the section owes (`contract`), the reply's format (`output`), the evidence rules, the citation rules — assembled in that order and joined by `PROSE_JOIN` (prose replies) or `LINE_JOIN` (one labelled field per line). `instructionOf` drops a part a step stated nothing for, so a step with no citation rules reads as an instruction about its own reply instead of one carrying an empty section. `labelledLines` states a labelled format as format lines (`ASSERTION: <one sentence claim>`), which is the D2 correction made reusable: a format line has to be answerable, not just describable.

**The extraction is a MOVE, and that is measured rather than asserted.** Before and after the move, a driven fixture run was captured with a recording writer (A1, A2, A3, B1, D2, D3 ×7 sections, D4 — twelve instructions in all), and the two captures are byte-identical. `tests/prompts.spec.ts` pins each instruction verbatim so a later change to one of them is a deliberate edit to a pinned string. No prompt's text changed by the move, so no version constant moved with it; `D4_AUTHOR_INSTRUCTIONS_VERSION` moved home to `prompts/d4-rebuttal.ts` unchanged, and `AUTHOR_INSTRUCTIONS_VERSION` no longer exists under that name.

**`accessNote` reaches the writer, and the instruction names it.** `SourceJudgement` carries `accessNote` (from `corpusSource(doi).accessNote`); `CitationUse` carries it too, because the citation records ONE reading and `bindJudgements` already carries `location` and `readScope` across; `citableSources` in `d3Executor` sends it in the roster, and `requestCitationsOf` records it in the request log beside the passage and its version. D3's instruction names the field and states the rule in the terms the sentence is judged on — attribution: "What a passage establishes is established by the text it was read in, so a sentence resting on that passage attributes the finding to the text you were shown — naming the version you read where the sentence would otherwise credit the cited work with words nobody read there — and where that cannot be said truthfully of the text you were shown, write less rather than more." `PROSE_REQUEST_VERSION` is `prose-request-v4`.

**Why both, and not one.** Material without a rule is the arrangement that failed first: `evidence-corpus.ts` recorded the passage, the reviewer's `rationale` and `scope` from the beginning, and a roster that carried the verdict alone asked for a sentence nobody could write. A rule without material is the arrangement that failed twice more: an instruction saying "state only what that source establishes" cannot be obeyed by a writer that has not read the source, and one saying "attribute the finding to what was read" cannot be obeyed by a writer that has not been told the two texts differ. The roster carries the fact and the instruction says what to do with it.

**`obligationsInstructionOf` moved to `argument-plan.ts`**, where the obligations it states are built, and returns its sentence without a trailing space: the skeleton's join supplies the separator, so the assembled D3 instruction is identical to the one the concatenation produced.

## Alternatives considered

- **Constrain the sentence in the instruction only.** This is the change that already failed twice in the same place. It also cannot be made specific: an instruction that tells a writer to attribute a finding to what was read, without saying which source's text was read in another version, asks it to be careful about a fact it does not hold. The run under repair is the measurement: the writer was told to state only what each source establishes, and wrote the plausible sentence anyway.
- **Carry `accessNote` into the roster only.** Refused as insufficient for the same reason in the other direction. `accessNote` reads as provenance ("read from the author preprint...; the DOI is the article of record"), which a writer can read as a note about the corpus rather than as a constraint on its sentence. Nothing else on the roster says what a citation may attribute — the existing scope clause exists precisely because a fact without a rule left the writer guessing.
- **Refuse the sentence mechanically, with a check.** Refused: attribution is a reading of what a sentence MEANS, and the citation layer's own rule is that final text is not scanned with regular expressions to recover meaning — a marker is the one thing a sentence carries as a typed inline value. No package-level check can decide it, so the constraint is stated to the writer and decided by the deployment's sentence assessor, which is where the objection came from.
- **Send the reviewer's `rationale` or `scope` instead.** Refused in the [passage note](2026-09-20-the-writer-is-shown-the-passage-it-cites.md), and still refused: the rationale says more than the passage does, which is how the run before this one was refused.
- **Ask the assessor to accept a preprint-sourced citation of the published article.** Refused outright, and not considered on its merits: the assessor is the gate, the objection is correct, and a change that argued it out of its verdict would be a change to the gate rather than to the request.
- **Give every prompt a version constant.** Refused as surface without a consumer. A version constant earns its place where something RECORDS it: D4's is stamped on the author's execution identity, and D3's record shape carries `PROSE_REQUEST_VERSION`. Five more strings nothing reads would be five things nothing checks, and the per-prompt pin is already `tests/prompts.spec.ts`.
- **One `prompts.ts` with seven exported constants.** Refused: the point of the move is that a step's instruction is findable and its rules are read from their owners; a single file of seven instructions re-creates the original problem in miniature. The modules also differ in kind — A1, D2 and D3 compose their text from the run's own facts, while A2, A3, B1 and D4 state nothing the run varies and their material travels as request context.

## Consequences

- **Measured on the deployment, 2026-09-20, two driven runs over `H:/dsh-boot-t30r`.** The fact reaches the writer: the session log the deployment wrote holds the request as sent, and the roster entry beside the one the assessor refused reads `"readScope": "full-text", "access": "read", "accessNote": "read from the author preprint (arXiv:2406.11508v1) of the published article; the DOI is the article of record", "source": "https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11508v1 (arXiv:2406.11508v1)"`; the instruction carrying the attribution clause is in the same log. Neither run was refused for crediting the cited work with words read in a preprint of it, and the second run's objection list was printed whole: NINE refusals, none of them about attribution. The first run's list was printed truncated at 400 characters, so only its first entry can be compared — `[statement-beyond-scope] 'stmt-conclusion-2' cites 'cite-10.1016/j.trb.2025.103352' … says nothing establishing the scope qualifier that the trade-off applies to coordinated CAVs among human-driven vehicles rather than to the single autonomous vehicle`, a scope qualifier and not an attribution. Both runs stopped at 12/16 with `D3-writing: in_progress`; the second run's first refusal is:
  > `[D3-writing] [statement-unrelated] 'stmt-introduction-1' cites 'cite-10.1109/tits.2026.3727135', which was assessed 'unrelated': The passage only concerns the reaction-time lag (a first-order lag) and the critical lag value above which safe CCC gains do not exist, saying nothing about a gap-to-closing-speed ratio, a minimum over a measurement window, or a platoon of one automated vehicle with two human-driven ones.`
- **What the runs do NOT settle, and what was not chased.** D3 still refuses, on the class it refused on before this change: a sentence that reports THIS study's method — its IDM platoon, its seeds, its equations, its own metric — while carrying a citation to a source that says nothing about them. That is the assessor being right about the sentences it read, it is not a defect this change introduced, and it was not chased: the assessor, the gate and the claim were left alone, and no threshold was moved to make a run pass. Two runs also cannot settle whether the attribution objection is gone rather than merely absent — a different model turn writes different sentences — so the claim recorded here is the narrower one the evidence supports: on these two runs, with the note in the roster and the clause in the instruction, the assessor did not raise it.
- **A prompt change is now a visible change.** Model-visible text is pinned verbatim in one spec, and each instruction sits in a module whose name is the step. A future change to a prompt that also needs a version bump has one place to make it.
- **`CitationUse` gained a field nothing checks.** `accessNote` on a binding is read by people, not by code: it is there because the citation record is what a reader audits and `location` alone names a URI without saying what that URI is relative to the work the key names. The cost is one more field to keep true in `bindJudgements`, which copies it from the judgement.
- **The D3 request is larger by the note's length** — roughly 200 characters per source for the two preprint-read sources in the shipped corpus. The corpus is fixed at three sources, so the growth is bounded with it.
- **A step whose prompt has no dynamic part exports the instruction itself** (A2, A3, B1, D4) while a step whose prompt names something the run supplies exports the function that composes it (A1, D2, D3). The asymmetry is the fact, not a missed extraction: the run's material for the other four travels as request `context`, and a function taking no arguments would say nothing a constant does not.

## Testing

- `tests/prompts.spec.ts` — the skeleton's assembly order, the absence of a part a step states nothing for, the labelled-format helper, and every one of the seven instructions pinned verbatim (including D4's author-instructions version).
- `tests/citation-binding-run.spec.ts` — the roster a driven run sends carries `accessNote` for each source, equal to the corpus's own note, and D3's instruction contains the clause that names the field.
- `tests/statement-bindings.spec.ts` — `judgementsFor` carries the corpus's `accessNote`; a judgement whose passage came from a preprint says so and says which DOI is the article of record; the recorded `proseRequests` entry carries the note; `proseRequestVersion` is `prose-request-v4`.
- Both extraction claims are measured outside the suite: a driven fixture run captured before and after the move produced byte-identical instructions, and the deployment run above read the fact back out of the session log.
