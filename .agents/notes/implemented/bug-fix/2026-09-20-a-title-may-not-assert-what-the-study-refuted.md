# Agent Note: A title may not assert what the study refuted, and D2's instruction now says so

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-09-20-a-title-may-not-assert-what-the-study-refuted.zh.md)

## Problem

The pre-send instruction check was turned on for a real deployment, and the run it read stopped at `D2-framework` with this refusal:

> [instruction-check] D2-framework: the instruction is missing the constraints on what may be asserted, which this task requires: The instruction carries its subject (the work described by the context's claim and methodFamily) and spells out the output format (exactly two lines, TITLE and SECTIONS with the seven ordered placeholders), but it states nothing limiting what the title and headings may assert

That is a finding about the instruction, not about the check. `frameworkInstruction` composed two parts — the task and the output format — and the skeleton's part for what may be asserted was absent, which `REQUIRED_INSTRUCTION_PARTS` is right to require: a step checked against "the parts it carries" is a step that passes on everything.

**The rule the instruction was missing already existed, in a module that reads what the writer produces.** `reviewManuscriptConclusion` in `runner/manuscript-review.ts` publishes the quality role `red-claim`, whose first check reads the direction words in the manuscript's **title** against the run's own record and refuses a title that states the direction the measurement contradicts. It exists because of a delivered paper: a headline beginning "Raising Minimum Time-to-Collision …" printed above a study that measured a 38.2 % fall, with a Results section reporting the refutation underneath it. The repository has a name for that defect, a regex that detects it, and a refusal that quotes it — and the instruction that asks a model to write the title said nothing about any of it, so the writer had to guess a rule it was never shown.

**The two are the same defect class this pipeline keeps meeting.** A rule enforced in one module and stated in another is how the D3 refusals arose: a roster without the passage, a passage without what was read. `red-claim` is one more instance, one step earlier, and the pre-send check is what made it visible while the run was still going rather than after it had stopped.

## Decision

**D2's instruction carries the skeleton's constraints part, composed from the run's own measurement.** `frameworkInstruction` now takes an `OutlineMeasurement` — the metric the study measures, the measured change in per cent signed the way the quality role reads it, what that change did to the claim, the direction the claim committed to and the magnitude it committed to — and assembles it as the instruction's `evidence` part, after the format and before the citation rules. The instruction's other two parts are unchanged, so the change is an addition and not a rewrite.

**The constraint is stated in the terms `red-claim` reads, at the strength `red-claim` applies, and no stronger.** The role refuses a title whose direction words contradict the record; the instruction says exactly that, and says that a title stating the direction the record found is what a title is supposed to do. A constraint that forbade direction words outright — "state no direction" — would refuse titles the check accepts, which would be a rule this pipeline does not have, and the writer would then be judged by two rules that disagree. The heading case is named too, because a heading is a claim like the title and is read the same way.

**The run's record travels in the instruction rather than in the request context.** `red-claim` compares the title against a signed number and a threshold, so the constraint states the signed number and the threshold: "a change of -38.2% in the minimum time-to-collision — it fell — against a claim that committed to an increase of at least 20%", followed by what that did to the claim. A claim that committed to no threshold says so rather than being reported as one that committed to 0 %. This is the lesson D3's refusals taught and this note does not re-derive: material without a rule and a rule without material are both arrangements that fail, and the second is the one D2 had.

**The outcome is the plan's own judgement, not a second one.** `d2Executor` reads it from `claimOutcomeOf` — the function `argumentPlanOf` calls, whose result is the `outcome` D3's obligations are built from — so a refutation stated to the title's writer and a refutation stated to the section writers cannot be two different facts about one run.

**`PROSE_REQUEST_VERSION` is `prose-request-v6`.** A request written before D2 stated its constraints is not read as one written under them.

**The check is turned back on in the deployment's profile, where it was left off.** It is a deployment setting (`Config.instructionCheck`, a `LlmInstructionChecker` on the prose route) and the shipped bundle leaves it unset, so a run under the bundle sends unchecked and records the absence. It was off while the D3 question was measured; a check the product cannot reach is not a check, so the deployment names it again.

## Measured on the deployment

- **The deployment's own reader clears D2's instruction.** The run cannot carry the instruction past A1 while the check is on (see below), so the reader was asked about it directly: the profile was booted, both instructions were composed by the same modules the steps compose them with, and `research.instructionCheck.check` was called with `REQUIRED_INSTRUCTION_PARTS` and the request contexts those steps send. D2's answer is `COMPLETE: true`, with the basis "The instruction carries the run's measured finding (-38.2% change in minimum time-to-collision, i.e. a fall) as the evidence to write from, the constraints on what may be asserted (a title or heading may state a direction only where the record found it, and an increase would be refused), and the exact output format".
- **A1 was the fidelity control, and it reproduced the refusal it was there to reproduce.** The same probe read A1's instruction — unchanged by this work — and answered `COMPLETE: false`, `MISSING: constraints on what may be asserted`, with the basis "The instruction names its evidence ("the following papers", supplied as the request's `papers` field) and its output format ("one gap per line. No numbering, no preamble, no closing commentary"), but its only stated limits are formal ones about the reply's shape, carrying no constraint on what may be claimed about the papers." A probe that could not reproduce a refusal the run had already made would not be measuring what the run measured.
- **Two real runs were driven with the check on, and neither reached D2.** The first stopped at `A1-landscape`: ``[instruction-check] A1-landscape: the instruction is missing constraints on what may be asserted, which this task requires: The instruction names the evidence (the context's `papers`, referred to as "the following papers") and fixes the output format ("one gap per line. No numbering, no preamble, no closing commentary"), but places no stated limit on what the writer may claim about those papers' o…`` — the log's own 400-character truncation, and the probe above holds the full text. The second run **passed A1 and A2** and stopped at `A3-agenda`: `[instruction-check] A3-agenda: the instruction is missing constraints on what may be asserted, which this task requires: The instruction draws its evidence from the context's claim/prediction and fixes the reply shape (PHASES/CONTRIBUTIONS blocks), but it names no limit on what the derived agenda or contributions may assert.` Neither refusal is about D2, both are correct statements about the instruction they name, and neither was answered by weakening anything.
- **What was NOT done to make a run reach D2.** A1, A2, A3 and B1 each state no constraint on what may be asserted, and giving four prompts their own constraints parts would have produced a run that reaches D2 by luck of which readings refuse. That is four prompt decisions, each about what a step of this pipeline may assert, and they are the caller's to make rather than this note's to take while a run is waiting to pass.
- **The same probe over all seven instructions gives the map of what the check now refuses.** Read against their real request contexts: `A2-claim`, `D2-framework`, `D3-writing` and `D4-rebuttal` are `COMPLETE`. `A1-landscape` is refused for "the constraints on what may be asserted" (the same part the first run refused it for). `A3-agenda` and `B1-method` are refused for "the evidence the task must be written from" — the reader holds that a claim and a prediction are the assertion to derive from and not evidence to write from, which is the same judgement D3's requests satisfy by carrying an `evidence` field. D2 is in the cleared set here and was refused in the run that started this work, which is the change this note is about; A1, A3 and B1 are the open findings.

## Alternatives considered

- **Have the check complete or repair the instruction.** Rejected in the [roster note](../feature/2026-09-20-a-roster-a-permission-and-two-bounds.md) and rejected again here for the same reason: an instruction that a reader rewrites is an instruction that differs between runs, and this layer's premise is that a claim nobody can re-read is a claim nobody can check. The check reads and refuses; the repair is a change to a prompt module, made and pinned like any other.
- **State the constraint in the request context and leave the instruction's text alone.** Refused: the refusal named the instruction — "it states nothing limiting what the title and headings may assert" — and the material it already had (the claim and the method family) travels in the context. A rule the writer is judged by belongs in the instruction, which is the text `tests/prompts.spec.ts` pins and the text a reader of the session log can re-read.
- **State the rule without the run's numbers.** Refused: "state the direction the record found, or state no direction at all" is not answerable by a writer that has not been told which direction the record found, and the constraint would then be one more rule stated in one place and decided in another. The numbers are the run's own and are the same ones the role compares the title against.
- **Forbid every direction word in a title.** Refused as over-strict: `red-claim` refuses a title that contradicts the record, and passes one that states what the record found. An instruction that forbade more than the check refuses would make the writer satisfy a rule nobody applies, and a run that obeyed it would print a title that states nothing.
- **Leave `red-claim` to say it at the end, and treat the pre-send refusal as noise.** Refused: `red-claim` reads the assembled manuscript, so by then the title is written, the sections are written under it, and the only remedies are a rewrite of the whole manuscript or a stop — which is the same "found by hand after a run stopped" pattern as the D3 defects.
- **Turn the check off again for this run, as it was off while D3 was measured.** Refused: a capability the product cannot reach is not a check, and the finding it produced here was correct. The check stays on, and the run stops at A1 with its reason quoted rather than being routed around.
- **Give each of the other prose steps a constraints part in the same change.** Refused as scope: A1's refusal is a real finding with its own decisions (what may be claimed about a set of papers' gaps is not what may be claimed about a run's own measurement), and answering four of them to make one run reach D2 would have been a change made to a gate's output rather than to the defect the gate named.

## Consequences

- **A headline can no longer contradict the record on the way in.** The rule `red-claim` applies at the end is now stated where the title is written, in the run's own numbers, so a run whose measurement refuted its claim is told the direction its title may state before it writes one.
- **The instruction is longer by roughly seven hundred characters**, on the request D2 sends once per run.
- **The pre-send check found real defects one step before the step it was aimed at, and the finding is open.** A1's and A3's instructions carry no constraints part. Both are recorded here and in the README rather than fixed, and until they are fixed a run under this deployment stops at whichever of the four the reader refuses first.
- **The reader's verdicts on one instruction are not stable across runs.** The same A1 instruction, unchanged, was refused by the first run and cleared by the second, and the probe refused it with a third phrasing of the same basis. That is a fact about a model-backed reader and not about the instruction, and it is recorded rather than smoothed over: the check fails closed, so a reader whose answers vary makes the pipeline stop more often than a deterministic one would, and no single run's "it passed" settles what the check thinks of an instruction.
- **What is not here**: nothing at D2 enforces the constraint. The instruction states it and `red-claim` decides it over the assembled manuscript; a title that contradicts the record in a way the role's regex does not read — a direction stated without any of its words — is refused by neither. The role's vocabulary is its own and this change did not widen it.
- **The shipped bundle still names no checker.** `Config.instructionCheck` is unset there, so the shipped runs send unchecked and record no check, which is a fact a reader can act on rather than a clearance; this note changes the deployment that measured the problem, not the default.
